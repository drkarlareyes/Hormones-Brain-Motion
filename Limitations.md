# Limitations

None of the four datasets was collected to answer the questions asked here, and each analysis is limited by what its dataset records.

1. **No measured hormone at the time of the outcome.** Only NHANES measures a hormone, once, without the cycle day. The other datasets give a self-reported cycle day (ds007615), an ovulation day estimated from home-recorded signs (Marquette) or nothing (CAPTURE-24). No analysis here measures a cycle effect directly.
2. **The Marquette ovulation day is an estimate.** It comes from a urinary hormone monitor or from cervical mucus, depending on the study arm, not from ultrasound or serum progesterone. The file does not label the arms; arm 0 is taken as the monitor arm only from its pattern of peak days. Any error in the estimated day adds to the error of the counting rules.
3. **The windows and the sample size factor of Figure 1 are choices.** A periovulatory window of 2 days before to 1 day after ovulation and a mid-luteal window of 5 to 9 days after it are common targets, but other windows give other hit rates. The factor 1/h² assumes that a session outside the window carries no luteal effect.
4. **The Marquette women were using fertility awareness methods for family planning.** Their cycles may differ from those of a general sample, for example in regularity, which would change the hit rates.
5. **ds007615 has one session per woman.** Contraceptive users and non-users differ in more than hormones, and the non-users mix past users (21) and never users (11). Contraceptive types differ in their progestin. The phase comparison uses only 27 to 29 non-users.
6. **The EEG pipeline is a simple, fixed one.** Eye artefacts were removed by regression on the electro-oculogram, without independent component analysis, and all measures come from an average reference and a posterior region of interest. Seven measures were chosen before the group comparison and corrected for; others (other bands, regions or connectivity) were not tested.
7. **The within-woman detectable effect in Figure 2 rests on an assumed correlation of 0.8** between sessions. A lower correlation would need more women.
8. **CAPTURE-24 covers about 24 hours per person and four broad classes.** The classifier uses 32 hand-crafted features and gradient-boosted trees without the temporal smoothing used in the CAPTURE-24 benchmark, so the absolute scores are not comparable with that benchmark. The training sets were matched for age band only; occupation, handedness and other differences between the sexes were not controlled.
9. **The confidence intervals of Figure 3 treat people as independent**, although test participants in the same split share the same training sets. Four of the 99 women were never drawn for testing.
10. **NHANES is cross-sectional.** Menopause status is self-reported, women after menopause were on average 9 years older than those still menstruating within the same 40 to 59 range, and the adjustment for age is linear. Hormone use comes from the prescription file only.
11. **The NHANES confidence intervals approximate the survey design** by clustering on the 30 strata-by-PSU units; they are not the full design-based (Taylor linearisation) variance.
12. **MIMS units and sleep-classified wear are summaries of wrist movement.** They measure the volume of arm movement and time classified as sleep by an algorithm, not locomotion or sleep itself.
